## Before You Start

</GamingSectionHeading>

<PillLegend />

<ZoneIntro accent="violet">
  Emulation itself is legal in most jurisdictions — it's the ROM files that carry legal risk unless you own the original media. The safest path is dumping your own cartridges/discs, but the community resources below reflect how most people actually approach this.
</ZoneIntro>

<WarnBox title="BIOS and firmware files are not optional extras">
Several emulators listed below (marked with a gear icon in the source material) require BIOS or firmware dumps to function correctly or at all — without them you'll get broken graphics, missing audio, or games that refuse to boot. These files are copyrighted, so sourcing them carries the same legal considerations as ROMs themselves.
</WarnBox>

## Multi-System Emulators

</GamingSectionHeading>

<ZoneIntro accent="violet">
  One emulator, many consoles. These are the best starting point if you don't want to juggle a separate program for every platform.
</ZoneIntro>

<InfoDeck>
  <InfoCard title="RetroArch" href="https://www.retroarch.com/" icon={<Layers />} tag="Frontend" tagStyle="emerald">
    The most widely used multi-system frontend, running dozens of emulator "cores" under one unified interface with shaders, netplay, and save states built in.
  </InfoCard>
  <InfoCard title="Multi-System Emulators List" href="https://emulation.gametechwiki.com/index.php/Multi-system_emulators" icon={<Layers />} tag="Index" tagStyle="violet">
    The Wiki's full comparison of every multi-system option beyond RetroArch, useful if you want alternatives.
  </InfoCard>
</InfoDeck>

<WarnBox title="Avoid these RetroArch cores">
The community consistently flags certain built-in cores as inferior to their standalone counterparts: avoid the **PPSSPP**, **Dolphin**, and **Citra** cores for PSP, GameCube/Wii, and 3DS respectively, and avoid the **Neo Geo**, **PlayStation 1**, and **Game Boy Advance** cores too. Use the standalone emulators listed below for those systems instead.
</WarnBox>

## ROM Sites

</GamingSectionHeading>

<WarnBox title="The .exe rule">
If a downloaded ROM file has a `.exe` extension instead of the expected format for that console (`.nes`, `.iso`, `.z64`, etc.), it is almost certainly malware. Legitimate ROM dumps never need to be "installed" — they're loaded directly into an emulator.
</WarnBox>

<InfoDeck>
  <InfoCard title="Myrient" href="https://myrient.erista.me/" icon={<Library />} tag="ROMs" tagStyle="amber">
    Widely considered one of the cleanest and most reliable ROM archives currently active, with a straightforward directory structure and no popup-laden download flow.
  </InfoCard>
  <InfoCard title="No-Intro" href="https://datomatic.no-intro.org/index.php" icon={<Library />} tag="Verification" tagStyle="violet">
    Not a download source itself, but the preservation project whose checksums verify that a ROM dump is clean and unmodified — cross-reference downloads against this when in doubt.
  </InfoCard>
  <InfoCard title="Vimm's Lair" href="https://vimm.net/" icon={<Library />} tag="ROMs" tagStyle="amber">
    A long-running, well-regarded archive with a restore-button script available if the download button goes missing.
  </InfoCard>
  <InfoCard title="CDRomance" href="https://cdromance.org/" icon={<Library />} tag="ROMs" tagStyle="amber">
    A solid general ROM archive with an active Discord for support when files or links break.
  </InfoCard>
</InfoDeck>

## Homebrew & Console Modding

</GamingSectionHeading>

<ZoneIntro accent="violet">
  Getting your own console to run custom firmware, homebrew apps, or emulators natively — distinct from PC emulation, but closely related in spirit.
</ZoneIntro>

<InfoDeck>
  <InfoCard title="cfw.guide" href="https://cfw.guide/" icon={<Wrench />} tag="Guides" tagStyle="rose">
    A conglomerate of community-written custom firmware guides covering nearly every console and handheld, the best single starting point for modding your own hardware.
  </InfoCard>
  <InfoCard title="ConsoleMods" href="https://consolemods.org/" icon={<Wrench />} tag="Wiki" tagStyle="rose">
    A community-driven modding wiki, strong on older consoles though it lags behind on the newest hardware.
  </InfoCard>
  <InfoCard title="NH Switch Guide" href="https://nh-server.github.io/switch-guide/" icon={<Wrench />} tag="Switch" tagStyle="amber">
    The definitive Nintendo Switch homebrew and CFW guide, covering RCM entry methods and safe modding practices.
  </InfoCard>
  <InfoCard title="WiiBrew" href="https://wiibrew.org/" icon={<Wrench />} tag="Wii" tagStyle="amber">
    The long-standing wiki dedicated to Wii homebrew development and installation guides.
  </InfoCard>
</InfoDeck>

<WarnBox title="RCM Jigs and soldering">
Entering RCM mode on a Switch to install custom firmware traditionally required soldering a jig by hand. It's strongly recommended to buy a proper RCM Jig instead of attempting solderless workarounds, which carry a real risk of damaging your console.
</WarnBox>
